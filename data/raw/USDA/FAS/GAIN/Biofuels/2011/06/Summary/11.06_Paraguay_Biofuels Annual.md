Report Highlights:
Paraguayan ethanol production for 2012 is projected up at 180 million liters. The sector is enjoying very good returns and
support through government policies. Contacts report that biodiesel does not receive the same level of support and has costs
of production that are higher than the cost of imported diesel. Production is expected to reach only 12 million liters in 2012.
Currently there is no local commercial plant producing biodiesel and production for 2011 is estimated at 1 million liters.
Post:
Buenos Aires
Executive Summary:
Paraguay’s ethanol industry continues to grow every year as a result of larger demand promoted by official policies,
including the biofuels Law. Local ethanol producers enjoy good returns, and continue to invest in expanding output and
becoming more efficient. Contacts indicate that today’s bottleneck is the limited acreage with sugarcane. The largest
ethanol producer currently utilizes corn as its main feedstock. A couple of new refineries are expected to come in line in
2012. Small volumes of ethanol could be exported starting in 2013.
