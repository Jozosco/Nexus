Approved By:
M. Riedel and J. Taylor
Prepared By:
Nguyen Huong
Report Highlights:
U.S. soybean meal exports to Vietnam in 2009 were a record 174,000 MT, rising 90 percent over the year before. Because
of exceptional growth and more emphasis on quality within the feed industry, Post expects U.S. exports of soybean meal to
double to another record of 350,000 MT in 2010.
The first commercial crushing facility in Vietnam will not begin operating at full capacity until the third quarter of
2011. Post expects total soybean meal imports to climb from 2.4 MMT to 2.6 MMT in 2010, and does not expect to see the
rate of soybean meal imports fall until the second half of 2011.
Executive Summary:
Vietnam’s oilseed production continued to fall well below demand from the food industry, the livestock and aquaculture feed
sectors and the vegetable oil industry. Accordingly, Vietnam plans to expand growing areas for major oilseed crops
including soybeans, peanuts, sesame, copra, sunflower and rice bran in 2010. However, Post doubts that production will
actually increase as much as the government would like it to due to the high input costs and low yields of oilseed crop
