Brazil currently accounts for only about one percent of global palm oil output, yet it holds significant
long-term growth potential, supported by favorable climate conditions, relatively strong labor and
regulatory frameworks, and availability of degraded pasture land for cultivation. Production is largely
concentrated in the Amazon region, mostly in the states of Pará and Roraima. The sector is projected to
expand steadily; however, growth remains constrained by high production costs, limited access to long-
term credit, reduced government support, and security concerns in key producing regions.
