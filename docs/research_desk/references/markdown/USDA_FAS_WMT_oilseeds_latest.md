#### Canada Canola Crush Raised to New Record

The 2025/26 Canada canola marketing year concluded at the end of July with Canadian government data conﬁrming new highs for the crushing sector. Crushing data from Statistics Canada came in at a new monthly record of nearly 1.2 million tons for June. Strong performance is also expected in July with the completed expansion of the Yorkton, Saskatchewan crushing facility, which doubled its capacity. Canada canola crush estimates for 2025/26 were raised 300,000 tons to 12.7 million tons to account for the recent strong crush performance.
For 2026/27, the Canada canola crush forecast is raised 500,000 tons to 13.7 million tons, attributed to strong demand, higher supply, and gains in canola crush capacity. As a result of many upgrades, expansions, and completion of new facilities over the past 3 years, crushing facilities in Canada have outperformed previous expectations. Motivating the increase in production is robust margins for canola crush. October contracts for canola crush on the Intercontinental Exchange are near $200/ton, a sharp (70 percent) increase compared to a year ago.
Strong domestic and international demand for canola oil is driving this strong crush. Domestically, Canada is using an increasing amount of canola oil for biomass-based diesel (BBD) production.
BBD consumption is rising domestically with substantial amounts also exported to the United States and the European Union. Additionally, record volumes of canola oil exports are forecast for 2025/26 and 2026/27. This oil is primarily exported to the United States and used for food manufacturing and BBD production.

The record crush is also producing record amounts of canola meal. The majority of this meal is exported, primarily to the United States. Exports of canola meal are estimated at 6.1 million tons in 2025/26 and 6.4 million in 2026/27, both records if realized. Feed consumption of canola meal in Canada has also grown rapidly. Canola meal can be used in most animal feed rations, with particular advantages for use in dairy and ﬁnﬁsh feed.
